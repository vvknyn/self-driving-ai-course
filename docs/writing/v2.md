# Writing and visual standard for Zero2FSD — v2 DRAFT (needs Vivek's OK)

v1 (approved 2026-10-10) stays in force: the six checks, the Never list, the worked example, the course-creator review. v2 adds what five teachers do, measured from real transcripts and video frames (scratchpad/transcripts/*/notes.md), and a visual standard.

Reader is unchanged: an adult with rusty high-school maths and no machine learning.

## A. Lesson shape (new)
7. **Open on a question from driving, then the goal.** The first 150 words name a concrete situation the learner can picture ("the car drifts left on a long bend — why?") and one sentence saying what they will be able to do by the end. [Feynman: car, wagon, water drop; 3b1b: the learner's question or a paradox, then the goal; MIT: objectives at the top]
8. **Show the finished result first, then rebuild it.** A figure or number from the working version comes before the lesson builds it. [Karpathy: micrograd's answer before any derivative; Feynman: the whole law in a sentence, then detail]
9. **Close the loop.** End with a 3-line recap and the question the next lesson answers, naming that lesson by title. [Ng: forward pointer in 19 of 20 videos; MIT: each clip ends on the next clip's question]

## B. Introducing an idea (refines v1 check 4)
10. **Order: situation → plain words → a number worked by hand → the symbol, read aloud → the name, last.** [all five: Ng builds J(w,b) one operation per sentence, symbol last; 3b1b names "integral" 10 minutes after showing it; Feynman's 5 → 5.1 → 5.001 before any Δ; Karpathy computes the slope at x = 3 before differentiating; MIT gives examples before the definition]
11. **Finite first, limit second.** Use a real step (0.05 s, one pixel, one metre) before any "as it gets smaller". [3b1b: dt = 0.01, 3 s vs 3.01 s; Feynman: "a millionth of a second"]
12. **One operation per sentence when building a formula.** Each sentence adds one operation and says why. [Ng: error → square → sum → average → ½ → only then J]
13. **Show the mess once, then the shortcut.** Expand one case in full, then say "you only ever do this once". [3b1b: t³ expanded at t = 2]
14. **Let the naive version fail first.** Show a plausible simple approach failing before the better one. [MIT 6.036: random hypotheses never improve, then the perceptron; Karpathy: the loss that blows up]

## C. Keeping the learner with you
15. **Predict before you look.** Before every figure or output that answers a question, one line asks for a guess ("Before you look: will the error grow or shrink?"). The answer sits under the figure. [Feynman: guess, compute, compare; Karpathy: "will d2 be slightly greater than four?"; Ng: predict, then run]
16. **Say each claim's status.** Mark what is optional, what is an approximation, what is a placeholder (and where it gets fixed, by title), and where confusion is normal. [Ng: 18 "optional" lines, 13 reassurance lines in 20 videos; Feynman: deducible / approximate / unknown; MIT: "I will talk later about what this gamma is"]
17. **Give a yardstick.** Each lab shows what a do-nothing or chance answer scores, so the learner can tell good from bad. [Karpathy: "a model that is completely untrained… 4% roughly"]

## D. Pace (derived, not measured on text)
18. **One new idea per 60–180 words.** Converted from measured speech: MIT dense clips 57–71 words per new concept, 3b1b about 122, Ng 85–176. This matches v1 check 1 (one idea per paragraph) when paragraphs stay under about 180 words.
19. **Do not copy Feynman's density.** His own Preface calls the approach "a failure" for most students. Borrow his order, not his pace.

## E. Visual standard (new)
20. **Density: about 5 visuals per 1,000 words, and never more than 300 words of reading without one.** Today: 1 figure per 605–745 words (4 per lesson). 3b1b: one held picture per 49–81 spoken words. Five per 1,000 words is a starting point to pilot, not a derived optimum. It means about 12 visuals in a 2,400-word lesson. Each panel of a build-up counts as one visual. Collapsed derivation boxes do not count as reading.
21. **A visual for every new idea.** The figure shows what the paragraph beside it names. One idea per figure; a second panel is allowed only for a zoom or the same quantity.
22. **One colour per quantity, course-wide.** A single palette module is imported by every `figures.py`, and the same hex goes into KaTeX `\color` macros. That way e_off is the same blue in the equation, the plot and the notebook. Proposed: e_off blue, ψ (heading error) pink, δ (steering) green, the perception box and its estimates coral. Gains get no hue of their own: a sweep over a gain is drawn as shades of the quantity it drives. Road paint keeps its real colours (yellow centre line, white edge), because the prose says "the yellow line" and the camera really sees it; yellow is used for nothing else. Road surface and axes are grey. Every colour is also named in words, for colour-blind readers. [3b1b does this within a scene; going course-wide is stricter than 3b1b, because our learner crosses lessons]
23. **At most 3 saturated hues per figure**, with everything else in grey. [3b1b: 164 of 187 sampled frames]
24. **Label on the picture, not in a legend.** Put names at the line ends, braces for distances, and axis titles in words with units. [3b1b: no boxed legends; replaces our 13 `.legend()` calls]
25. **Build up in steps.** Small multiples on the same axes, adding one element per panel, with earlier elements faded. Use a short animation only for continuous motion. [3b1b: in-place build with dimming]
26. **Visual types to use.** Pick from this menu:
    - function plot;
    - two named curves;
    - colour-graded family (a sweep);
    - geometry diagram;
    - number line;
    - top-down coordinate plane;
    - side-by-side states;
    - zoom inset;
    - braces and arrows;
    - pixel grid with values;
    - equation built term by term in colour;
    - faded earlier state;
    - recap thumbnail of the last lesson;
    - loop block diagram (camera → box → steer → move), with the active box lit;
    - opening question card.
27. **Interactive where a number changes the story.** On the site, a slider steps through precomputed sim runs (e.g. k_off over the gain sweep), so no simulation runs in the browser. In Colab, an ipywidgets slider can do the same, but this is untested in our labs. The static PNG always stays as the fallback.
28. **Every figure is generated by code from the sim.** No hand-drawn figures. The derivation rule applies: every number on a figure comes from a run.

## F. Gates (QA, enforced by tests in the course repo)
- No stretch of more than 300 words in `lecture.md` without a visual.
- No colour literal in any `figures.py` outside the palette module.
- Every palette hue has ≥3:1 contrast on the figure background (computed).
- Every line that a label or the prose names can be told apart from its neighbours: each label sits on its own curve, never stacked at a shared end point (pilot defect, Oct 10).
- Every figure is referenced by the prose next to it (alt text reads as a caption — v1).
- No dotted ids in learner-visible text (lecture, lab, quiz, cards, grader messages).
